## Introduction

RealtyJuggler is a CRM built specifically for real estate agents. RealOrganized, Inc., its maker, markets it to new agents, individual experienced agents, and teams. It can also be used by a broker for an entire office of agents.

RealOrganized is a small, privately held company based in Fort Collins, Colorado. It began in 2003, when software engineer Scott Schmitz, who had worked at Apple and AOL, joined forces with Davette Everly, a top-producing agent near Washington, D.C., who wanted simpler agent software. The product went on sale the following year. More than two decades later, Schmitz remains president and directs development, working with a small staff, most of whom have been with the organization for over 10 years. In September 2026, he published “Real Estate CRM Secrets,” a book that shows first-year agents how to build a business around a CRM. A sequel, “Real Estate CRM Mastery,” is scheduled for release in the first quarter of 2027 and is geared toward more experienced agents. Both books aim to explain how a real estate CRM can boost business and make an agent more productive. Most agents use only a fraction of the functionality available in their CRM.

RealtyJuggler COM focuses on an easy-to-use product, with personalized support and training, all at an affordable price. The product is offered at a single pricing tier that includes onboarding, coaching, and live phone support.

## Fit

RealtyJuggler is best suited for new agents, individual agents working independently, agents with an assistant, and real estate offices.

A new agent gets an affordable system to carry them through a lean first year, a long free trial to learn it, and a real person to call for setup, coaching, and help along the way. The tools for meeting new people and following up with them are ready to use, along with a large stock of ready-made content, including eCards, Letters, and flyers, so there is little to build before getting to work.

An experienced agent working alone gets nearly everything needed to run a business built on past clients and referrals while still gathering and incubating new leads. Leads from websites and portals arrive without retyping, and the system maintains a steady rhythm of staying in touch with a sphere of influence on schedule. The affordable price makes this CRM a great fit for a part-time or semi-retired agent. Don’t be fooled by the low price into thinking the product lacks features. Its feature set is quite competitive with CRMs costing many times its price.

An agent can share their database with an assistant or an entire team. Features include assigning tasks, tracking progress, and delegating routine work such as mailings, follow-ups, and keeping deals on track. A change log and the option to restore deleted or altered records help protect the agent's business while the assistant works in the database.

A broker can use RealtyJuggler to let each agent track and incubate their own leads while the broker supervises the entire office. Each agent sees only the leads assigned to them. The long free trial suits brokers who may experience high agent turnover. The broker pays for seats only after the 90-day trial ends, so the office doesn't pay for agents who leave soon after arriving. Training and phone support come with every membership, easing the ongoing work of bringing new agents up to speed.

One issue reviewers have noted is the simple user interface, which can look dated. A second limitation is the 10,000-record limit per agent, which makes RealtyJuggler a poor fit for a high-volume team. However, for agents for whom these two limitations are not a factor, RealtyJuggler is well worth considering.

## Features

**Score: 8.7/10**

RealtyJuggler includes nearly all standard real estate features in its single plan.

RealtyJuggler offers a well-balanced set of features, including a fully featured transaction manager, client service reports, robust print and email marketing, and financial tools for tracking income, expenses, goals, and projected closings. Team controls are unusually detailed for the price, and leads from portals and websites flow in without re-entry, thanks to both email parsing and customized CSV importers. The weaknesses lie in automation and in limits on the total number of records the CRM can handle. These matter little to a solo agent working a sphere of influence or even a small team. For a high-volume team, these limitations will make the CRM less appealing.

### Privacy Policy

**Score: 10/10**

RealtyJuggler's privacy policy treats an agent's contacts as private information, prohibits selling or reusing them, and allows the agent to export the data at any time.

The [Privacy Policy](https://www.realtyjuggler.com/Privacy) states plainly, "We will not sell or rent Member Private Information or Member Account Information," and clarifies that the vendor acts only as a processor of that information, using it to provide the service. AI features are similarly constrained: "Insights derived from one Member's data are never used to train, inform, or improve outputs" for other members, and nothing is pooled across accounts.

The protections are built to last. Any change that expands the use or sharing of a member's data, or weakens a protection, requires that member's opt-in consent. The policy would bind a buyer of the company, a commitment the [Terms & Conditions](https://www.realtyjuggler.com/Terms) repeat.

After a membership ends, the account remains readable for at least 60 days, and its contacts and prospects can still be exported as CSV files. On request, the vendor permanently deletes the data. RealtyJuggler does not build or host agents' websites; leads that arrive from an agent's own site become part of the agent's contacts and fall under the same policy.

The Privacy Policy's section on changes contains two versions of the same paragraph. The first requires a member's opt-in consent only before broader use applies to data already collected, leaving room to apply new terms to data added later; the second, fuller version requires consent whether the data was collected before or after the change and also covers changes that weaken a protection. The fuller version is clearly the intended rule, and the company should remove the older paragraph so that members know which one governs.

The policy's list of sharing purposes opens by stating that both account information and members' private information are used for every purpose on the list, including sales support and announcements of new features and services. The Data Controllership section that follows limits the use of members' private information to providing the service, keeping it secure, and meeting legal requirements, and assigns the other purposes to account information only. The later section states the intended rule, but the opening sentence of the list should be narrowed to match it.

> - +1: The policy's proactive statement that end-customer data is private, not resold, not shared, and not used for AI training
> - +1: It cannot be changed to be less favorable without opt-in consent
> - +1: A new owner is bound by it
> - +1: The vendor handles end-customer data only as a processor
> - +1: Export after the membership expires or is canceled

## Conclusion

RealtyJuggler is a CRM built exclusively for real estate and is a good fit for new agents, individual agents, and agents with an assistant. A real estate office can run every agent on it. High-volume teams and franchises that need site licensing may be tempted, but should probably look elsewhere.

RealtyJuggler’s strengths include a broad set of useful tools in a single plan, terms that let an agent try it at length and leave at any time, a privacy policy that keeps clients' information private and portable, and personal help from setup through daily use, as reviewers on every site confirm. Its weaknesses include a dated look, limited automatic handling of incoming leads, and a database size ceiling that most agents are unlikely to notice.

No critical red flags were identified. The company is long established, still run by its founder, and has no mortgage, title, or brokerage business that could compete for an agent's clients.

With setup, training, and support included in a low annual fee, RealtyJuggler is an excellent value for the agents it is built for, and an office pays the same low rate for each agent.
