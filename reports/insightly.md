## Introduction

Insightly is a customer relationship management system for business-to-business sales. It is not designed specifically for real estate agents. Its maker pitches it to small and mid-sized companies in fields such as professional services, manufacturing, and financial services, particularly to firms whose relationship with a customer continues after the sale while the work is delivered. Real estate agents, teams, and brokerages are not among the buyers it targets.

The company was founded in Perth, Australia, in 2009 by Anthony Smith and has been based in San Francisco since 2012. In July 2024, it became part of Unbounce, a Vancouver-based landing-page software maker owned by the private equity firm Crest Rock Partners. Unbounce's chief executive, Steve Oriola, now oversees both businesses.

It promises a single system that a growing company can use to manage prospects, close sales, and organize the work that follows, tailored to its own process rather than a fixed industry template, with per-user pricing aimed at smaller firms that have outgrown spreadsheets yet don't want a complex and expensive enterprise system.

## Fit

Insightly isn't a good choice for real estate agents, teams, or brokerages because it was built for businesses in general and lacks built-in support for how agents find, win, keep, and serve clients. A company prepared to invest in customization could shape it into a workable real estate system by adding its own follow-up routines for buyers, listings, and closings, but that takes time and likely paid help. Some everyday parts of an agent's work, such as texting clients and taking in leads from real estate websites, would still be missing. There is no real estate-specific content library, such as flyers, eCards, and letters. Because the product focuses on selling to businesses, Insightly doesn't handle real estate-specific tasks like tracking open houses, listings, showings, and commission calculations. Real estate CRMs provide these things ready to use, so agents and offices of every size will get further, sooner, with one of them.

## Features

**Score: 4.9/10**

Insightly's features score is respectable for a CRM not designed for real estate.

Its strength is the core of a sales system. Contact and company records are detailed and linked to one another and to deals, with roles and pipelines. Pipelines are flexible, and reporting is unusually deep, with standard dashboards for pipeline value, rep rankings, and lead sources, plus custom reports that compare periods. Two-way email and calendar sync, a mobile app, web forms that create leads, and a full set of team controls, from shared records to role hierarchies, permission profiles, and automatic lead assignment, round it out.

The gaps are in all real estate-specific features. There is no texting, no printing of letters, labels, or envelopes, no connection to listing data, no lockbox, showing or offer tracking, and no agent calculators. Lead intake from real estate portals and IDX websites is missing, and the content an agent would send to clients, such as letters, drip campaigns, flyers, and cards, is not available in the product.

## Terms of Service

**Score: 9/10**

Insightly's pricing is published and simple, with three plans. The 14-day free trial runs at the recommended Professional level without a credit card, so a trial cannot turn into a charge by accident. Customers can pay month to month or choose annual billing for a discount, according to Insightly's help article on [managing a subscription](https://documentation.insightly.com/hc/en-us/articles/51917527502996-Managing-your-subscription-billing).

The commitments that follow a purchase are more binding than the trial suggests. Under the [Terms of Service](https://www.insightly.com/terms-of-service/), a subscription renews automatically for the same term unless the customer gives at least 30 days' notice before it ends, and renewals are charged at the then-current list price. Reviewers on Google Business Profile, Capterra, and the App Store report charges after canceling and accounts they could not close. Insightly's help article warns that its self-service cancel button closes the account permanently, so a customer who only wants to stop billing has to contact Insightly instead. Disputes are subject to arbitration in San Francisco under Delaware law.

The contract leans toward Insightly on money. A customer who stops before the end of a term still owes the remaining fees and receives no refund. The same bill comes due if Insightly ends the agreement due to the customer's breach. Insightly can also end it immediately, without notice, if the customer violates its Privacy Policy. Money flows back only if Insightly itself commits a breach and fails to fix it. Neither side can walk away mid-term without cause. On monthly billing, the obligation is small, but a customer who chose annual billing for its discount is committed for the full year.

Recent changes have cost current users some functionality. Insightly's own [article about its mobile app](https://documentation.insightly.com/hc/en-us/articles/51913213342356-Insightly-CRM-Mobile-App) says the June 2026 rebuild omitted six features from the previous app that customers had been using. One Trustpilot reviewer in 2026 said the price "doubled" at renewal, but we found no other recent source confirming a price increase.

## Support

**Score: 9/10**

Insightly's support score reflects what it offers on paper. Phone and email support are included with every plan. Insightly's [pricing page](https://www.insightly.com/pricing-plans/?plan=crm) states that all users "can reach our support team by phone or email," and its help article on [contacting support](https://documentation.insightly.com/hc/en-us/articles/51913699017620-Contacting-Support-Submitting-a-Ticket) explains that the phone line is accessed from inside the app. No support number appears on the website's contact or home pages.

The self-help material is extensive. The [help center](https://documentation.insightly.com/hc/en-us) was rebuilt in mid-2026 and contains about 200 articles. The [Insightly CRM YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/@InsightlyCRM/videos) has more than 450 videos, and a webinar library is linked from the help center.

Hands-on help costs extra. [Guided Onboarding](https://documentation.insightly.com/hc/en-us/articles/51913661061524-Onboarding-Success-Plans), four to six Zoom sessions with an onboarding specialist covering configuration, data imports, reports, and admin training, is sold separately. The [Premier Support and Success](https://www.insightly.com/premier-support-and-success/) plan, priced as a share of license fees, adds individualized training, a customer success manager, a priority queue, and longer phone hours (6 to 6 Pacific time on weekdays, instead of 9 to 4). Data migrations and custom integrations are handled by Professional Services under a statement of work and billed on a time-and-materials basis; none of the help Insightly describes, paid or free, includes building reusable importers for a particular lead source's files. Nothing in Insightly's written policy limits support during the 14-day trial.

The support score sits far above the review score of 2, and the gap is the key thing a buyer should understand about Insightly. The written policy is generous, yet the most consistent complaint across Trustpilot, Google Business Profile, TrustRadius, and the app stores is slow or missing support, with replies taking days and tickets going unanswered. Customers on the Premier plan with a named success manager tend to describe a very different experience from those who rely on the standard queue.

> **How the score was calculated:** Starts at 7, then:
>
> - +1: Phone support is included with all plans
> - +1.5: Onboarding, 1-on-1 training, and help importing data, all available for an extra charge (0.5 each)
> - −0.5: The phone number is not listed on the contact or home page
>
> 7 + 2.5 − 0.5 = **9**.

### Company

**Score: 4.5/10**

Insightly, Inc. is an established general-purpose CRM provider that has been part of a private-equity-backed marketing software company since 2024.

Anthony Smith founded Insightly in Perth, Australia, in 2009 and moved it to San Francisco in 2012 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insightly)). In July 2024, it merged into Unbounce, the Vancouver landing-page company owned by Denver private equity firm Crest Rock Partners ([Crest Rock Partners](https://www.crestrockpartners.com/news/insightly)). Unbounce's CEO, Steve Oriola, now also leads Insightly, and Mike Cravens is VP of Product ([Insightly webinar, Aug 2025](https://www.insightly.com/blog/resources/webinar-2025-product-updates/)). Unbounce does not sell another CRM, and we found no ties to real estate brokerage, mortgage, or title businesses.

The product is still being developed. Since the merger, Insightly has shipped a steady stream of AI, email, and integration updates, most recently in October 2026. It rebuilt its mobile app in June 2026 and rewrote its help center in mid-2026. At the same time, support and billing addresses moved to Unbounce domains, and reviewers describe a decline in service after the change in ownership. Employees see the strain too: Insightly is rated 3.4 out of 5 from 100 reviews on [Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/Reviews/Insightly-Reviews-E654775.htm), and the three most recent, all from 2026, describe frequent shifts in direction and a weaker culture since the merger.

### Privacy Policy

**Score: 9.5/10**

Insightly's contract treats the contacts and records an agent stores as the customer's own, to be used only to run the service and deleted once the account is canceled.

The [Terms of Service](https://www.insightly.com/terms-of-service/) state that the customer owns its CRM data and that Insightly "will not access your CRM Data for any purpose other than in connection with the provision of Services." The [Privacy Policy](https://www.insightly.com/privacy-policy/) does not address that data at all; it describes Insightly as processing information under its clients' direction, with use limited to providing the service the client engaged it for.

The AI tools are covered as well. The Privacy Policy states that data obtained through Insightly's connection to outside AI assistants is not used to train AI models, and that customer data is not used to train publicly available models. When an agent connects an outside AI assistant, the data it retrieves is governed by that assistant's terms, so agents should review those terms before connecting.

Deletion is promised in the help center rather than in the contract. Insightly's article on [managing a subscription](https://documentation.insightly.com/hc/en-us/articles/51917527502996-Managing-your-subscription-billing) states that data is retained for two weeks after cancellation as a grace period and then deleted. It also notes that the self-service cancel option deletes the account and all of its data at once, with no way to recover it.

Insightly's documents conflict on what happens to data when a subscription ends. The Terms of Service allow Insightly to delete a customer's data within 24 hours of expiration or termination and offer an export only on request in the ten days before the termination date, while the help center promises a two-week grace period after cancellation during which an administrator can reinstate the account. A customer cannot tell which rule applies, and Insightly should state a single retention period in both documents; until it does, agents should export their data before canceling.

A help-center [notice about the Unbounce merger](https://documentation.insightly.com/hc/en-us/articles/51913699069716-Insightly-Unbounce-Merger-Your-Data-Sharing-Options) states that customer data is shared across the Insightly and Unbounce brands for purposes including personalized recommendations, cross-selling, and joint marketing, with an opt-out available by email. The notice does not specify whether customer data refers to Insightly's own account holders or to the contacts and records agents store. The Terms promise that Insightly will not access CRM data except to provide the service. Read together with the Privacy Policy, which leaves the data customers store outside its scope, the notice appears to cover account holders only, but Insightly should state that plainly in the notice itself.

> **How the privacy policy score was calculated:** Starts at 7, then:
>
> - +0.5: Stating that customer data is private and not used to train AI models
> - +1: Insightly handling that data only as a service provider
> - +1: Committing to deleting the data after an account is canceled
>
> 7 + 2.5 = **9.5**.

## Conclusion

Insightly is a generic business CRM rather than a real estate system, and it suits no type of real estate buyer well: agents, teams, and offices would have to adapt it to their work at their own expense and would still lack some of the everyday tools a real estate CRM provides.

It does well at the core business of selling. Most users find it quick to learn; it handles shared work and supervision across a team effectively; the subscription is moderately priced, and its written terms are fair regarding trials, month-to-month billing, support, and the privacy of the records customers store. What holds it back is the distance between that general design and real estate work, and the gap between the company's written promises and what customers describe in practice, where slow or absent help, billing trouble, and lingering problems come up again and again, more so since the 2024 merger.

Two red flags deserve attention. Support is generous on paper but, according to many independent accounts, slow to respond in practice. And a customer's data can disappear quickly when a subscription ends because the company's own documents set different rules for how long it is kept; anyone leaving should export everything first.

As a purchase, the subscription is reasonable for what it does, but the paid setup and support a real estate buyer would likely need to adapt it would raise the first-year cost several times over, and the result would still be a general tool. Insightly is a sensible choice for a small business outside real estate; agents and brokerages will get more for their money from a CRM built for their work.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does Insightly cost?

Insightly's typical plan, Professional, costs $588 a year, less than the median of $1,044 a year across the 26 CRMs we scored. Its plans run from $348 a year (Plus) to $1,188 a year (Enterprise). Add-ons such as Guided Onboarding, Premier Support & Success Plan (20% of license, $3,000 minimum per year) and Insightly Marketing (Plus) cost extra.

### Does Insightly have a free trial?

Yes: 14 days, with no credit card needed. The longest trial we found is 90 days: see [the CRMs with the longest free trials](https://thecrmcomparewebsite.github.io/the-crm-compare-website/blog/longest-free-trial-crm.html).

### Who is Insightly best for?

Insightly is a general business CRM, not one built for real estate, so an agent has to set up lead routing, transactions and past-client follow-up themselves.

### Is Insightly a good CRM for real estate agents?

Insightly scores 6.4 out of 10, #8 of the 26 CRMs we scored. Its best category is terms of service (9) and its weakest is reviews (2). Insightly is a generic business CRM rather than a real estate system, and it suits no type of real estate buyer well: agents, teams, and offices would have to adapt it to their work at their own expense and would still lack some of the everyday tools a real estate CRM provides.

### What do customers say about Insightly?

Across 314 ratings on 11 review sites, Insightly averages 3.7 stars, and 69% of ratings are four or five stars. Users find it easy to learn, but slow or missing support, billing trouble, unfixed bugs and a large share of low ratings dominate the open review sites.

### Does Insightly have a mobile app?

Yes. Native iOS and Android apps; rebuilt in June 2026 with six features (such as audio notes) temporarily unavailable, and no offline mode.

### Does Insightly connect to the MLS?

No. No MLS or IDX listing import found; listings would have to be entered or imported into a custom object.

### Can you text clients from Insightly?

No. No built-in texting; SMS is available only through third-party apps such as RingCentral's Insightly app.

### Can I import my contacts into Insightly?

Yes. CSV/Excel import with field mapping, duplicate checks and update-by-Record-ID for contacts, leads, organizations, notes and tasks.

### Who makes Insightly?

Insightly, Inc., an Unbounce company, based in San Francisco, California, United States.

### What are the best alternatives to Insightly?
