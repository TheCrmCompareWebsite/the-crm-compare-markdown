## Introduction

Referral Maker CRM is a contact manager designed exclusively for real estate agents. It is published by Buffini & Company, a real estate coaching and training firm founded in Carlsbad, California, in 1995 by Brian and Beverly Buffini. Buffini introduced the CRM in 2012 and rebuilt it in 2019. Brian Buffini still owns the company and now serves as its chairman; Darin Dawson, previously its chief revenue officer, has served as chief executive since April 2026.

Buffini targets the product at solo agents and at agents who work with an assistant or a small team and grow their business through repeat clients and referrals rather than purchased internet leads. Agents can buy the CRM by itself, but it also comes with every Buffini coaching and training membership, and much of its marketing invites CRM buyers to step up into those programs.

Its appeal lies in its method rather than a long list of tools. Referral Maker is the software version of Buffini's Work by Referral approach: it determines whom the agent should contact each day and aligns that daily routine with the income the agent wants to earn, so agents who already follow Buffini's teaching get a CRM that works the way they were coached to work.

## Fit

Referral Maker CRM is built first for the established solo agent and then for the agent who has hired an assistant.

An experienced solo agent whose next deals will come from past clients and friends gets a CRM that, each day, outlines whom to call, write to, and visit, and ties that habit to the income the agent is aiming for. It is simple to learn, support is easy to reach, and a single agent can pay for it month to month at a modest price.

An agent with an assistant can grant that assistant access to the entire client list for a small additional fee while retaining control of the account. The assistant can handle routine follow-ups and mailings while the agent is out with clients. The trade-off is control: the assistant can change or delete anything in the shared list, and work cannot be delegated to the assistant within the CRM.

Both should weigh the timing. Buffini is moving its method to another company's platform and has not said when every member will get it or what agents who buy the CRM alone will pay afterward, though it has said its top membership will cost far more. Signing up now means learning software that is about to change underneath you, using a mobile app that hasn't been updated in a long time. Either kind of agent should expect to revisit the choice once the new platform arrives.

## Features

**Score: 2.6/10**

Referral Maker CRM has a narrow but well-defined feature set, and its public documentation covers only part of the product.

Its strengths lie in tools for building a sphere of influence. Relationship records store personal details, and the system ranks and groups each relationship. The system turns those rankings into reminders and a daily list of people to call, write to, and visit. Goal tracking, referral tracking, activity reports, and a simple pipeline show whether the agent is on pace. Email and print campaigns reach a single relationship, a group, or the whole database. The mobile app dials clients directly and logs the call, and a team account lets an agent share the whole database with an assistant or team members.

The weaknesses are on the lead-generation and operations sides. Buffini documents no automatic intake of leads from email, Zillow, or a web form; no Zapier connection; no texting; no drip sequences; no two-way contact sync; and transaction tracking stops at deal values and income. Team controls are thin: team members can be prevented from exporting the leader's contacts, but anyone on the team can change or delete any shared record. Buffini's help center is open only to members, so many rows below could not be verified and score nothing; the table separates those unverified rows (blank) from features confirmed to be missing (0).

Several features earn half credit because they cost extra, are part of a higher plan, or are only partially available. Video email, powered by BombBomb, is a $29-a-month add-on. The monthly eReport newsletter and the done-for-you marketing kit are preloaded only for Referral Maker PRO and coaching members, and Buffini will print and mail those pieces for an added monthly fee, available only with PRO Personalized. Envelopes can be printed for mailings, but label printing is not documented. Team leaders can assign leads and review each member's contacts, transactions, and goals, but they route leads by hand. Google Calendar syncs with the app, though users have noticed delays, and reviewers report no link to Microsoft 365.

The Pop-By map is the feature that sets Referral Maker apart. The app uses the agent's location to show nearby ranked clients, so agents can schedule a visit between appointments and log it on the spot, earning Niche credit. The goal engine is similarly distinctive: an agent enters an income target, and the CRM works backward to determine the number of calls, notes, and visits needed each week.

Two higher-tier extras are worth noting, but they earn no credit on the typical plan. Social Suite, powered by Back At You, automates social media posting and pulls listings from most MLSs, but it is available only to PRO and coaching members. The Buffini Referral Network, a directory for exchanging referrals with other Buffini agents, is included with PRO and coaching but costs extra for CRM-only members.

## Terms of Service

**Score: 6.5/10**

For Referral Maker CRM, a 30-day trial and month-to-month terms work in the agent's favor, but the cancellation and termination rules favor Buffini.

The product page still offers a free 30-day trial by phone, although the online trial link now leads to the Referral Maker PRO page. Every membership under Buffini's [Terms & Conditions](https://www.buffini.com/company/terms/) runs month to month and renews automatically until canceled; prepaying for a year is optional. Prices are published, but the CRM is included in six memberships, which makes the choice harder than it needs to be.

Leaving takes longer than a month's notice suggests. Canceling "shall require thirty days' advance notice," and the cancellation only takes effect on the last day of the month in which those 30 days end, with no refund for the final or partial month. An agent who gives notice early in a month can pay for nearly two more months, which is exactly what several Yelp reviewers describe. Agents who prepay a year and leave early are refunded only after the months used are re-priced at the higher monthly rate.

The Terms are lopsided. An agent must give 30 days' notice and pay through the end of the month in which that notice expires, while Buffini may end a membership without cause on five days' written notice. The Terms state that termination for any reason does not change the fee owed for the rest of that month, and they offer no refund of a prepaid year when Buffini ends it. The Terms also allow Buffini to change or discontinue features at any time without notice.

Change is on the horizon. Buffini plans to run its system on [BoldTrail + Buffini Mode](https://www.buffini.com/solutions/boldtrail/), a platform built by Inside Real Estate, and says no member will need to switch. The [Referral Maker PRO page](https://www.buffini.com/solutions/referral-maker-pro/) already states that the PRO plan will increase from $149 to $349 per month when the new platform launches, with today's sign-ups retaining the lower rate. Buffini has not said what will happen to agents who buy the CRM alone.

>
> - +1.5: A 30-day free trial
> - +0.5: The shortest term is monthly
> - −0.5: Complex pricing (six memberships include the CRM)
> - −0.5: The announced increase in the Referral Maker PRO price
> - −0.5: The cancellation notice can run well past 30 days
> - −1: Buffini can end a membership without cause and keep that month's fee
>
> 7 + 2 − 2.5 = **6.5**.

Reviews largely support these promises. Those complaints cluster around the mobile app and cancellations rather than everyday questions.

## Governance

### Company

**Score: 8/10**

Buffini & Company is a long-established, founder-owned coaching company with leadership and technology both in flux.

Brian Buffini and his wife Beverly founded the company in 1995. It is based in Carlsbad, California, with a second office in Colorado Springs, and calls itself the largest real estate coaching and training company in North America. Referral Maker CRM is a side product of the coaching business: it exists to put Buffini's system into practice, and most of its marketing directs agents toward paid memberships. The company is not part of a larger group and does not operate a brokerage or lender.

Leadership has changed quickly. Dermot Buffini, chief executive since 2013, stepped down in January 2026, and Brian Buffini returned as chief executive. In [April 2026,](https://press.buffini.com/buffini-company-names-darin-dawson-ceo-buffini-brian-buffini-transitions-to-chairman/) Darin Dawson, the company's chief revenue officer, became chief executive, and Brian Buffini became chairman. In June 2026, Dawson added a new chief revenue officer, chief operating officer, and chief ambassador. Dawson co-founded BombBomb, the video messaging company that powers Referral Maker's paid video add-on, and two of the new executives also came from BombBomb.

The CRM's direction is the main concern. In May 2026, Buffini announced that its system will run on Inside Real Estate's BoldTrail platform, which it describes as the new engine behind its method. By September, an early version was being tested by a small group of members who also hold BoldTrail accounts and work from their Referral Maker contacts ([Inside Real Estate](https://help.insiderealestate.com/en/articles/15687169-boldtrail-coming-soon-buffini-mode)). But six months after the announcement, Buffini still lists no launch date or pricing. Meanwhile, Referral Maker itself shows signs of stagnation: the iPhone app was last updated in March 2025, the Android app in February 2025, and the most recent product announcements concern BoldTrail rather than Referral Maker. Agents should treat Referral Maker as a product being wound down toward a successor, even though Buffini has not announced an end date.

### Privacy Policy

**Score: 8.5/10**

Buffini promises not to sell the information agents store about their clients and allows agents to have it deleted on request.

Buffini's [Privacy Policy](https://www.buffini.com/company/privacy-policy/) identifies the information agents enter about their clients, such as names, family members, addresses, birthdays, and social media handles, as one category of personal information it holds. For that client information, it lists only three purposes: maintaining and servicing the agent's account, providing customer service, and analyzing its products to improve them.

The policy's central promise is clear: "We will not sell your personal information to anyone." It adds that Buffini will share personal information only as needed to provide or improve its services, or in aggregated form that does not identify anyone. The companies that help run the service, such as those that process payments, deliver messages, and back up data, may use the data only on Buffini's instructions and may neither sell it nor use it for their own marketing.

Agents can ask Buffini to delete their information, and Buffini says it will also instruct the companies it has shared the information with to delete it. Billing records and similar business records are retained after a deletion request.

The weak point is what happens at the end of a membership. The [Terms & Conditions](https://www.buffini.com/company/terms/) allow Buffini to permanently delete an agent's data when a membership ends and state that Buffini has no obligation to keep, export, or return it. A returning member reported on Yelp in 2025 that the data in his old Referral Maker account was gone. Agents should export their database before giving notice.

Members who move to BoldTrail + Buffini Mode will be bringing their contacts to a platform operated by another company. Buffini's BoldTrail page states that the database remains the agent's and is not shared with third parties, but it doesn't specify which privacy terms will govern client data on the new platform, so members should ask before migrating.

One sharing clause uses the word client in a way that conflicts with the rest of the policy. After listing the routine reasons for sharing, the policy says Buffini works with real estate, mortgage, and real estate servicing companies and may share "the personal information of a client" with a related business of that client, such as a franchisor or principal, whenever Buffini decides there is a legitimate business purpose. Read alongside that list of franchisors and principals, the clause appears to refer to Buffini's own members and their brokerages. Elsewhere, however, the policy uses Client Information to mean the people agents enter into the CRM, so an agent could reasonably read the clause as allowing Buffini to pass those clients' details to mortgage or brokerage partners. Buffini should state plainly which information the clause covers and exclude agents' client information from it.

A second ambiguity concerns marketing. The policy's table limits Client Information to maintaining the account, customer service, and product analytics. Still, the next section says Buffini may use all categories of information, to the extent applicable, for purposes that include promoting its offerings and sending marketing messages. The table's narrower list is the more natural reading for agents' clients, but the general wording should specify the categories it covers so the two sections cannot be read against each other.

The end-of-membership rules also conflict with the Privacy Policy's rights section. The policy allows an agent to request a copy of their personal information, including the client information they store, up to twice a year in a portable form, and it does not say that this right ends with the membership. The Terms, however, state that Buffini has no obligation to hold, export, or return an agent's data after cancellation or termination and may delete it permanently. Buffini should state whether the copyright survives the end of a membership and, if so, for how long, so agents know whether they can still retrieve their clients' records after they leave.

> **How the privacy policy score was calculated:** Starts at 7, then:
>
> - +0.5: The policy states that personal information, which it defines to include agents' client information, is not sold and is shared only as needed to provide the service
> - +1: Buffini commits to deleting data on request
>
> 7 + 1.5 = **8.5**.

## Conclusion

Referral Maker CRM is a real estate-specific CRM designed for established agents, with or without an assistant. It serves these customers well, provided their business comes primarily from past clients and referrals, and they are willing to follow Buffini's daily routine.

What it does well is narrow but useful. It turns a referral habit into a short list of daily actions that agents find easy to maintain. Technical support is excellent, membership is month-to-month, and its privacy terms promise that agents' client records will not be sold. However, there are limitations. It offers little to agents who depend on internet leads and relies on a mobile app that has frustrated users for years. Reviewers report difficulty canceling memberships, with billing continuing for weeks after an agent gives notice.

The largest red flag concerns the product's future. Buffini is switching its technology underpinnings to Inside Real Estate's BoldTrail platform. The new product is reportedly priced higher than the current product. It is unknown what the user interface or features will be once the transition is complete. The disruption and uncertainty make any recommendation of Referral Maker problematic. Add to this the lack of recent updates to the existing product, and current customers are feeling squeezed. Management at Referral Maker has changed twice in the last few years, and leadership changes add to the uncertainty. ReferralMaker should be treated as software in transition.

At its price, it is fair value rather than a bargain: the cost is moderate, and so is what it delivers. It makes the most sense for referral-minded agents already in Buffini's coaching who will use it as intended. Agents who rely on online leads or need firm control over an assistant will find more complete options. Anyone who signs up should plan to reconsider when the new platform arrives.

- **[RealtyJuggler](realtyjuggler.md)** *(our score: 9.8/10)*: A low-priced real estate CRM with strong reviews and some of the most customer-friendly contract and privacy terms in this comparison. It covers the same sphere and referral work as Referral Maker, with a far broader feature set, and includes phone support, onboarding, and training, for about $179 a year. However, it looks dated and does little to automate online leads.

Compared with Referral Maker, it adds lead capture and automation for about $499 a year, though its trial requires a card and its terms allow no refunds.
- **[IXACT Contact](ixact-contact.md)** *(our score: 6.7/10)*: An easy, sphere-focused real estate CRM that handles much of an agent's client marketing using vendor-written content, under a contract that keeps client data private and deletes it on request. It suits the same referral-minded agents as Referral Maker for about $561 a year, but its support has been hard to reach, and its owner, Elm Street Technology, now also sells the competing Grove.
- **[Follow Up Boss](follow-up-boss.md)** *(our score: 6.5/10)*: An easy-to-learn lead-conversion CRM owned by Zillow, built for agents with an assistant and for high-volume teams that work a steady stream of online leads. It is the stronger choice for agents who buy leads at about $696 a year, but its privacy terms allow its owner to use, share, and market many of its clients' records, with no way to opt out.
- **[Realvolve](realvolve.md)** *(our score: 6.3/10)*: A highly configurable real estate CRM for detail-oriented agents who want to shape every relationship and transaction process, best run by an agent with an assistant or a team with staff. It offers far more automation and deal management than Referral Maker, with strong privacy promises, for about $790 a year. However, its automation draws reliability complaints, and its support is available only in writing.
- **[Grove](grove.md)** *(our score: 6.2/10)*: A simple, low-cost CRM that Elm Street Technology launched in November 2026 for solo agents who work a sphere of past clients. Like Referral Maker, it centers on relationships rather than lead buying and costs about $600 a year. However, it is brand new, has no reviews yet, and can’t be used by a team yet.
- **[Lofty](lofty.md)** *(our score: 5.1/10)*: An all-in-one real estate platform, formerly called Chime, that pairs a CRM with agent websites and automation for high-volume teams working a steady flow of fresh leads. It does far more for online leads than Referral Maker, but it costs about $3,588 a year under a one-sided, year-long contract with no trial. Reviewers report slow support and buggy apps.
- **[Top Producer](top-producer.md)** *(our score: 4.8/10)*: A long-running real estate CRM for experienced agents who work a sphere, owned by Constellation and split into two product versions. It covers more ground than Referral Maker but costs about $2,148 per year on a non-refundable one-year term. Its reviews have turned sharply negative since 2025.
- **[BoldTrail](boldtrail.md)** *(our score: 4.5/10)*: A broad, actively developed platform from Inside Real Estate, built for franchises and large brokerages that license it at scale, and the technology behind Buffini's announced BoldTrail + Buffini Mode. It does far more than Referral Maker, but it costs about $5,988 a year, has no trial, unpublished pricing, auto-renewing contracts, and poor reviews.
- **[CINC](cinc.md)** *(our score: 4.3/10)*: A lead-generation service with a capable CRM, built for high-volume teams that buy online advertising, and owned by Fidelity National Financial, which also sells title, mortgage, and insurance services. It is the opposite of Referral Maker's referral approach, costing about $11,988 per year for one agent before any advertising budget, under a one-sided contract and privacy terms.
