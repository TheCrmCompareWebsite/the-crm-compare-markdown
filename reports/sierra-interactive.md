## Introduction

Sierra Interactive is a real estate specific platform that pairs a search-optimized IDX website along with a CRM built to work with the leads those websites capture. The company can help build the website and manage advertising to generate leads.

It includes support for MLS listing feeds, listing alerts, lead routing, and a built-in dialer. The company also runs Google and Facebook ad campaigns for its clients. The entry package is for solo agents and small teams, while the larger packages and most of the marketing target growing teams and brokerages that want their website to be their main source of business.

The company traces its roots to 2003, when founder Ben Peskoe began building real estate websites in the California ski town of Mammoth Lakes. Sierra Interactive was formally founded in 2007 and adopted a subscription model with its own CRM in 2017. Based in Louisville, Kentucky, it joined Alpine Software Group, backed by the private equity firm Alpine Investors, in 2022, when Peter Maglathlin became chief executive. Its main appeal is a single system where an agent's branded, search-friendly website and the CRM share the same lead data, so the agent who follows up can see every visitor's searches and visits.

## Fit

Sierra Interactive is best suited to high-volume teams and real estate offices.

A high-volume team gets a lead engine built around its own website. Search-friendly property and neighborhood pages attract buyers and sellers, and the CRM shows what each lead is browsing, routes new leads to the right team member, and automatically starts follow-up, so a team that already spends on advertising can run its website, ads, and follow-up through one vendor. An inside sales agent on a team put it: "Behavioral triggers and lead activities make it so much easier to identify opportunities" ([Google Business Profile, 2026](https://www.google.com/maps/place/?q=place_id:ChIJxzP5it5yaYgRe6tPbn5JfJ4)).

A brokerage can put the office and every agent on a single branded platform, with agent websites under the office's name, oversight of each agent's activity, and recruiting tools, while preventing agents from accessing one another's clients. Seat prices fall steeply as the office adds users, so the platform is far more affordable at office scale than its single-user price suggests.

An agent with an assistant can share one database, limit what the assistant sees, and bring in new prospects to work. It falls short of ideal because the system is built for working fresh web leads rather than for keeping in touch with past clients and the sphere, where most of this agent's business comes from. Much of the everyday communication toolkit costs extra in the entry package.

Any buyer should weigh the yearlong agreement with no free trial, customer reports of slow support, and privacy terms that offer little protection for client data.

## Features

**Score: 4.7/10**

Sierra Interactive has a strong lead-capture and team foundation, with real gaps in follow-up, transaction, and print tools.

The platform is strongest at the intersection of the website and the CRM: listings flow directly from agents’ MLS feeds, registered visitors become leads with their browsing history attached, and teams get the permissions and oversight a shared database requires. However, much of the day-to-day communication toolkit is an upgrade to the entry-level package, and the CRM offers little support once a lead becomes a client.

## Terms of Service

**Score: 2.5/10**

Sierra Interactive's terms are held back by the lack of a free trial and a yearlong commitment that customers can’t easily leave, while the vendor retains broad rights to end the relationship.

There is no free trial; prospective customers book a demo and sign up from there. Both billing options include a 12-month agreement: paying monthly does not make the plan month-to-month; it only raises the rate and adds a setup fee ([Realty Tech Hub](https://realtytechhub.com/sierra-interactive-review/)). Neither the pricing page nor the [Terms of Service](https://www.sierrainteractive.com/terms-of-service/) explains what happens when that year ends, and no renewal term is published. A long-time customer on Reddit reported receiving two weeks' notice that month-to-month contracts were moving to annual terms at a higher price ([Reddit, 2024](https://www.reddit.com/r/realtors/comments/ung5qv/anyone_use_sierra_interactive_before/)).

The balance of power favors the vendor. Sierra may terminate or suspend an account "for any reason whatsoever" without prior notice, and paid subscription fees are non-refundable. As a result, a customer whose account is closed has no stated right to a refund. Sierra may also change its fees at the end of any billing cycle after giving notice. Reviewers who tried to leave early report being held to their contracts, which the agreement allows.

One point favors the customer: the agent retains ownership of the website's domain name, and Sierra manages only its DNS while the site is hosted ([help center](https://help.sierrainteractive.com/helpcenter/how-domains-dns-and-support-requests-work)). Disputes are governed by Kentucky law.

>
> - −2: No free trial
> - −1: The vendor's right to end an account for any reason and retain prepaid fees
> - −1: The 12-month minimum plan term
> - −1: The lack of a published renewal term, which is treated as 12 months
> - +0.5: Customers retain their domain name when they leave
>
> 7 + 0.5 − 5 = **2.5**.

### Privacy Policy

**Score: 6/10**

Sierra Interactive's privacy policy contains little that puts agents' client data at risk, but it makes almost no commitments to protect it.

The vendor's [Privacy Policy](https://www.sierrainteractive.com/privacy-policy/) is primarily intended for visitors to Sierra's website and for agents who hold accounts. The agent websites that Sierra builds link to the agent's own privacy policy, so leads who register on those sites are covered by the agent's terms rather than by Sierra's.

The one passage that addresses CRM data concerns email. The policy states that some versions of the CRM are built on Context.IO technology and that its providers, DokDok and its parent Return Path, may access "your information" and use it under Return Path's privacy policy, with an opt-out available on Context.IO's website. Because that technology reads connected mailboxes, which hold the agent's conversations with clients, it would allow an outside company to use client correspondence for its own purposes.

For Google-connected email and calendars, the policy is more stringent: Sierra stores only conversations between the agent and contacts, does not add contacts to other mailing lists, and will delete that Google data on request. No similar promise covers the rest of the database. Neither the policy nor the [Terms of Service](https://www.sierrainteractive.com/terms-of-service/) says that client data stays private, that Sierra handles it only to provide the service, that agents can export it after leaving, or that it will be deleted when the account ends. Sierra may change the policy at any time, effective when posted.

The policy also allows Sierra to share information about its customers and former customers among its affiliates. In context, this covers agents' account information rather than their clients. Still, with Homebot, a service sold to loan officers among its sister companies, the policy does not rule out sharing client data within the group.

The Terms contradict themselves about ownership of agents' stores. The Content section says customers retain all rights to the content they post. Yet, the next paragraph states that content found on or through the Service is the property of Sierra Interactive LLC. The Termination section says ownership provisions survive the end of the agreement. Sierra should state plainly that leads, contacts, and notes belong to the agent.

The Context.IO paragraph names outside companies and an opt-out site but does not specify which CRM versions use the technology. Sierra should state whether any current version shares mailbox data with Context.IO's providers and remove the clause if none does.

> **How the privacy policy score was calculated:** Starts at 7, then:
>
> - −1: The wording does not explicitly name client data as allowing Context.IO's providers to use information from connected email accounts under their own privacy policy (half of the 2-point deduction)
>
> 7 − 1 = **6**.

## Conclusion

Sierra Interactive is a real estate IDX Website and CRM platform built for high-volume teams and brokerages. For that audience, its website, lead capture, and team controls are genuine strengths.

However, the weaknesses are serious. The price is high for a single agent, the entry package charges extra for calling, texting, and the mobile app, and the tools for nurturing past clients and managing transactions are limited. There is no free trial, the agreement runs for a full year, and the terms give the vendor far more room to walk away than the customer. The written support policies are among the most generous available, yet reviews have noted slow support, overselling of the product, and unresolved bugs. Recent reviews have grown more critical.

The privacy terms pose no direct conflict of interest, but they offer little assurance regarding client data, and the weak BBB grade warrants caution. For a team or brokerage with an advertising budget and the time to build out its website, Sierra Interactive is worth considering, provided the promises made during the sale are put in writing. For individual agents, it is poor value.
