## Introduction

Apptivo is a general-purpose business CRM, not one built for real estate agents. It is sold as part of a suite of online business applications for small and mid-sized companies across every industry. Its website lists real estate among the many industries it serves, not as a focus.

The company has sold Apptivo since 2009 and remains privately held. Its founder, Bastin Gerald, still serves as chief executive.

Apptivo's appeal is its breadth of features at a modest per-user price. Businesses can tailor the system to their way of working without hiring a programmer.

## Fit

Apptivo is a general business CRM and is not an ideal choice for anyone buying a CRM for real estate work. It is sold to businesses of every kind and lacks the real estate tools agents rely on, such as tracking a sale from contract to closing, importing leads from real estate websites and portals, and ready-made marketing content for agents. An owner willing to invest the time could shape it for real estate work with custom fields, automation rules, and templates, but that requires considerable setup; some work would still have to happen in other software, and the result would remain a do-it-yourself system you would need to improve and maintain. Buyers should also consider that Apptivo’s development has slowed considerably over the last few years and that the company seems to be in maintenance mode.

## Features

**Score: 5.3/10**

Apptivo is strongest in the tools every sales team needs. Leads, contacts, customers, and deals live in separate but linked apps; email and calendars sync both ways with Office 365 or Google Workspace, and automation rules create tasks, send emails and texts, and route new leads to the right person. Team controls are unusually deep for the price: an owner decides who can see which records and fields and who may export or delete; every change is recorded with the old and new values, and deleted records can be restored.

Its gaps are almost entirely on the real estate side. Apptivo's documentation provides no support for transactions, contingencies, showings, or listing deadlines; it offers no way to import MLS listings or portal leads, and it includes no letters, flyers, eCards, or real estate calculators. Apptivo also states that it cannot merge duplicate contacts and does not provide printed mailings, such as labels and bulk letters.

## Terms of Service

**Score: 10/10**

In Apptivo's terms, a free trial without a credit card, month-to-month plans, and the freedom to cancel anytime outweigh a crowded plan lineup.

New customers get a 14-day trial of the Ultimate plan without entering a credit card, and the trial does not roll into a paid subscription. Apptivo emails plan options before the trial ends ([Pricing FAQ](https://www.apptivo.com/app-pricing/)). Every plan can be paid month to month, and the vendor tells buyers they are "not bound by any contracts or commitments." Four paid plans split the features among them.

Leaving is straightforward. Under the [Terms and Conditions](https://www.apptivo.com/terms-and-conditions/), a monthly account that requests cancellation is not billed again and remains active until the end of the period already paid for. Money already paid is another matter: fees are non-refundable, and discounted annual plans are never refunded, so an agent who prepays for a year has committed to it. The refund complaints described in Reviews show customers running into this. Two reviewers say they were billed after canceling, too few to show that Apptivo ignores its own cancellation terms.

The balance of power tilts toward Apptivo in one respect. A customer who prepays for a discounted annual plan cannot recover any of it, even when leaving early. Yet Apptivo may close an account on its "reasonable belief" that the terms were broken, and that closure can include deleting all of the account's data. Nothing in the terms returns prepaid fees in that case.

A few clauses deserve attention. If a card payment is declined, Apptivo may make all unpaid fees immediately due and suspend the account. The agreement is written as a one-year term that renews automatically, though the same document allows a customer to end it at any time simply by not using the service. Apptivo may delete the data of an account left unused for 180 days, may change the terms by posting a new version, and sends disputes to individual arbitration in California.

### Privacy Policy

**Score: 10/10**

Under Apptivo's privacy commitments, customers own their data; Apptivo says it shares no client data with third parties; and its data processing addendum limits its handling of that data to the customer's instructions.

The [Privacy Policy](https://www.apptivo.com/privacy-policy/), last updated in July 2026, states that "Apptivo does not provide any of our client data to third parties" and that clients own their data, which Apptivo does not intend to use. Customers may import contacts, which Apptivo may use only to provide the requested service. The policy also prohibits using customer data for advertising.

The [Data Processing Addendum](https://www.apptivo.com/dpa/), which forms part of Apptivo's terms of use, designates Apptivo as a "Contracted Processor" that processes the customer's personal data only on the customer's documented instructions. The [Terms and Conditions](https://www.apptivo.com/terms-and-conditions/) state the same: Apptivo will not use content stored in an account for commercial or marketing purposes without permission, and will access it only when necessary to provide the service.

Leaving is also well provided for. Apptivo's [Data Security page](https://www.apptivo.com/data-security/) states that client data "can be deleted on demand at any time" via a self-delete feature, with business data cleared from backups shortly afterward. The addendum also allows a former customer to request a complete copy of its data in writing after the service ends.

Much of the policy's broader language, about sharing information with affiliated companies and integration partners and sending newsletters and offers, describes how Apptivo treats the account holder's own details rather than the contacts an agent stores. Leads that arrive through Apptivo's web forms land in the agent's account, where the client-data commitments apply.

The addendum's scope is unclear. It is titled a European data protection addendum, yet it states that it forms part of the terms of use and defines the data protection laws it follows to include those of any other country where they apply. Apptivo should state plainly whether its processor, return, and deletion commitments cover every customer.

The documents also disagree on how quickly data is deleted. The Data Security page states that business data is cleared from rolling backups shortly after an account is deleted. At the same time, the addendum sets an outer limit of six years after the service ends. Apptivo should provide a single deletion period that applies to all customers.

> **How the privacy policy score was calculated:** Starts at 7, then:
>
> - +1: The data processing addendum makes Apptivo a processor acting only on the customer's instructions
> - +1: Apptivo commits to deleting client data on request
> - +1: A former customer can have a complete copy of its data returned after the service ends
> - +0.5: The policy states that client data is private, not shared, and not sold (three of the four protections)
>
> 7 + 3.5 = 10.5, above the top of the scale, so **10**.

## Conclusion

Apptivo is a general business CRM, not a real estate product, so it is a poor fit for every kind of real estate buyer, from a new agent to a franchise: whoever chooses it has to design their own real estate processes and keep part of the work in other software.

It offers what you’d expect from a well-run small-business suite: straightforward month-to-month terms and a free trial with no credit card required. The company is an established, founder-led company. Every plan includes live help, and most customers find the product easy to use and well supported. Its documents clearly state that customers own their data and can have it deleted or returned. On the downside, it lacks the real estate depth agents need; many users find the interface dated, and a consistent minority report billing disputes after canceling and unresolved issues.

The clearest warning sign is the lack of momentum. Product announcements stopped at the end of 2024, and recent releases have focused on maintenance, giving little reason to expect the product to evolve to meet agents’ needs.

Judged on price, Apptivo is good value, but for most agents, much of that value lies in business tools they will not use. It makes sense mainly for someone who runs a broader business alongside real estate and is prepared to build a real estate workflow manually.
