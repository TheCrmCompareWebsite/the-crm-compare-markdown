## Introduction

Rethink is a real estate-specific CRM with a narrow focus: it is built for commercial real estate brokers. It runs as an application on top of Salesforce and is marketed to commercial brokers, their teams, and entire brokerages working in investment sales, leasing, tenant representation, and capital markets. Buildout sells it per user after a sales demo, alongside its other commercial real estate tools for marketing listings, managing transactions, and researching properties.

Rethink was founded in Austin, Texas, by Vijay Mehra, and commercial brokers have used it since at least 2012. Buildout, a commercial real estate software company based near Chicago, acquired it in February 2021. Chief executive Helen Calvin leads Buildout.

Rethink's appeal is that it speaks the language of commercial brokerage from day one. Instead of adapting a general sales tool, a commercial broker or firm gets a system organized around buildings, spaces, owners, tenants, and deals, with Salesforce's security and reporting underneath and a direct link to the Buildout tools many commercial firms already use to market their listings. Buildout markets it only for commercial real estate, so residential agents are not its audience.

## Fit

For commercial real estate, Rethink is an ideal fit for an individual broker, a broker working with an assistant, and a commercial brokerage office.

An individual commercial broker gets a system that works the way commercial deals do, tracking the buildings, owners, and tenants behind each relationship and the long, multi-step deals that follow, rather than a residential tool bent to fit. Buildout describes Rethink as "built by brokers, for brokers" ([Rethink by Buildout](https://www.buildout.com/platform/rethink-by-buildout)). For a broker who manages a book of property relationships over many years, that focus is why they choose it.

A commercial broker with an assistant gets one shared database where the broker decides what the assistant can see and change, and work can be handed off and followed up on while the broker is out with clients. The assistant can also handle the upkeep a configurable system like this requires, turning one of Rethink's demands into a natural part of the job.

For a commercial brokerage, Rethink is closest to the buyer Buildout has in mind. The broker supervises the office, with each agent's records kept private and shared only where the firm allows, plus office-wide reporting on pipeline and commissions. Agents get a system built around their own deals. An established firm with someone to run the system will get the most; an office with constant turnover will feel the lack of live training and have to do more setup work.

All three face the same hesitations. Rethink cannot be tested before purchasing. Its contract renews automatically, and sales quotes are included in its price. Its recent reviews are scarce and unhappy, and its owner now sells a newer CRM of its own alongside it. New agents, high-volume teams, franchises, and anyone in residential real estate are poorly served.

## Features

**Score: 5.2/10**

Rethink has a deep set of property, deal, task, and team tools on a Salesforce foundation, offset by the lack of built-in texting or calling and limited print or residential marketing content.

The strongest areas are those a commercial brokerage relies on. Contacts, properties and listings live in separate, linked records; deals have their own pipelines, key dates and documents; tasks, action plans, touch cycles and daily call lists are prebuilt; and the Salesforce sharing model lets a broker supervise agents while keeping their records private. The platform includes a change log and undelete, and niche tools such as stacking plans, a map-based property search with a tour builder, and public property sites set it apart from residential CRMs.

The gaps show up for anyone who works with residential leads. We could not confirm automatic intake from residential lead sources. Drip email and a library of ready-made letters, eCards, and newsletters are thin, and printed mailings are scarce. These matter less to a commercial broker, but they hold the score in the middle of the range.

## Terms of Service

**Score: 2.5/10**

Rethink's terms of service are weak: there is no free trial, the contract term is not published and renews automatically for the same length, fees are nonrefundable, and part of the price is not disclosed.

Buyers cannot try Rethink before committing; the only way in is to book a demo. The [Master Subscription Agreement](https://www.buildout.com/terms-of-use) leaves the length of each subscription to the order form and renews it for terms "equal in length to the expiring Initial Term" unless either side gives written notice at least 30 days before it ends. It also states that all fees paid are nonrefundable and offers no price lock at renewal. Buildout publishes no standard term length, but Rethink's own FAQ said licenses followed Salesforce's requirement for "annual prepayment of licenses" ([Rethink FAQ, archived](https://web.archive.org/web/2021/https:/www.rethinkcrm.com/faq)). A 2026 reviewer who tried to cancel was held to the contract, so buyers should expect a yearly commitment they cannot leave early.

Because Rethink runs on Salesforce, customers also accept [Salesforce's pass-through terms](https://www.salesforce.com/en-us/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/documents/legal/Agreements/alliance-agreements-and-terms/OEM-Pass-Through-Terms.pdf), which limit them to the Salesforce functions described in Rethink's user guide and rule out refunds upon termination. Disputes go to the courts of Cook County, Illinois. The plan prices are published, but two charges listed under Price have no published amounts, so the full cost is known only after a sales conversation.

The agreement also leans toward Buildout. A customer can leave only at the end of a term. In contrast, Buildout may end the agreement immediately, without the 14-day chance to fix a problem that otherwise applies, if it reasonably believes the customer has breached the terms governing the use or ownership of client data. Nothing in the agreement refunds the fees already paid for the rest of the term. Buildout may also suspend access on 10 days' notice for any breach, including a late payment, while the customer keeps paying, and it may charge a fee to reconnect.

> **How the score was calculated:** Starts at 7, then:
>
> - −2: No free trial
> - −1: The shortest term is not published and is therefore treated as 12 months
> - −1: Renewals run for the same length as the expiring term
> - −0.5: Pricing is hard to find (platform maintenance and onboarding fees are not published)
>
> 7 − 4.5 = **2.5**.

### Privacy Policy

**Score: 7/10**

Rethink customers can download their data for 30 days after leaving, but Buildout retains a permanent license to that data.

The rules for client data are set out in Buildout's [Master Subscription Agreement](https://www.buildout.com/terms-of-use), which defines client data as everything submitted by or for the customer. That includes contacts, deals, and notes, as well as inquiries clients send back through the Property Sites after a broker emails them. Buildout's US privacy policy, on the same page, covers visitors to buildout.com and users of its app rather than this data.

The agreement lets customers download their data, including attachments, as a CSV file if they request it within 30 days after the agreement ends; after that, Buildout says it has no obligation to retain or provide it. That is not a promise to delete it, and the weak point is the license customers grant over their data. The agreement gives Buildout a "perpetual" license to host, copy, display, and use client data through its services, to keep backup copies, and to train the AI features built into its services, with nothing stating that this use stops when a customer leaves.

One inconsistency in the agreement should be corrected. The license is labeled perpetual, and the intellectual property section that contains it is listed among the sections that survive termination. Yet the license covers using client data "by means of the Services" and performing Buildout's obligations under the agreement, both of which end when the subscription does. The data retrieval clause then says only that Buildout has no obligation to keep the data after 30 days. As written, a customer cannot tell whether Buildout may continue using a former customer's data; Buildout should state either that the license ends with the subscription or what continues and for how long.

The same license allows Buildout to sublicense, transfer its rights, and distribute client data, but it names no one who may receive the data and gives no purpose beyond the service itself. A sentence limiting sublicenses to the companies that host and run the service for Buildout would remove any doubt about whether client data can pass to anyone else.

>
> - −1: The vendor may retain customer data for its own purposes after cancellation
> - +1: Customers can export their data for 30 days after canceling
>
> 7 + 1 − 1 = **7**.

## Conclusion

Rethink is a real estate CRM designed for selling commercial real estate. It is best suited for individual commercial brokers, brokers working with an assistant, and commercial brokerage offices. Residential agents are not its intended audience. Buyers get a system that understands their work, but they should weigh its purchase terms and the owner's limited commitment to improving the product before signing.

Its strengths are depth and control. It organizes property relationships and long deals the way commercial brokers work, and its Salesforce base provides robust supervision and reporting that few smaller systems match. Its weaknesses are reach and upkeep: it does little for the fast lead follow-up that residential agents depend on, much of its flexibility requires an administrator to unlock, and its reviews, once warm, are now few and mostly unhappy.

The clearest red flags include a price revealed only after a sales conversation, no free trial, and a lengthy minimum contract term. Also noteworthy is that the parent company now sells another competing CRM alongside Rethink. The terms of service and privacy policies are also concerning, as Rethink retains a perpetual license to your data, even after you cancel your membership. Rethink still receives regular software updates, but recent reviews note a mismatch between what the product promises and what it delivers.

Judged on features for the price, Rethink is below average in value. It makes most sense for a commercial broker who can commit annually and is willing to invest in the steep learning curve. Anyone who needs a free trial or whose business is residential should look elsewhere.
